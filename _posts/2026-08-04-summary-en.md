<summary><span class="hz-item-title">Aggregate-then-Calibrate: A Two-Stage Framework for Human-Centered Assessment with Guarantees</span> <span class="hz-item-score">⭐️ 7.0/10</span></summary>

**Problem:** Human-centered assessment tasks lack verifiable ground truth and face a dilemma: human-only methods suffer from heterogeneous expertise and inconsistent scales, while model-only methods rely on imperfect proxies or incomplete features. Existing approaches fail to effectively combine these complementary sources.

**Method:** The paper proposes Aggregate-then-Calibrate (AtC), a two-stage framework. Stage-1 aggregates heterogeneous comparative judgments into a consensus ranking using a rank-aggregation model that accounts for annotator reliability. Stage-2 calibrates any predictive model's scores via isotonic projection onto the order, enforcing ordinal consistency while preserving quantitative information.

**Results:** Theoretically, the paper shows that modeling annotator heterogeneity yields strictly more efficient consensus estimation, isotonic calibration enjoys risk bounds even under misspecified consensus ranking, and AtC asymptotically outperforms model-only assessment. Empirically, on semi-synthetic and real-world datasets, AtC consistently improves accuracy and robustness over human-only or model-only assessments.

**Significance:** This work bridges judgment aggregation with model-free calibration, providing a principled recipe for human-centered assessment when ground truth is costly, scarce, or unverifiable. The theoretical guarantees and consistent empirical gains highlight its potential to improve decision-making in domains relying on subjective human judgment.
