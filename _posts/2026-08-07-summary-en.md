The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) has released its twentieth data release (DR20), featuring an all-sky map of half a million supermassive black holes, a 3-to-4-fold expansion in SMBH data over DR19. The map includes quasars and active galactic nuclei, providing a comprehensive view of these objects across the sky. This release significantly advances our understanding of supermassive black holes and their distribution, which is crucial for cosmological studies and tests of structure formation models. The large catalog will enable researchers to probe the growth and evolution of black holes and their host galaxies, and it complements other surveys like eROSITA's X-ray catalog. The map is based on spectra from SDSS-V, which integrates facilities capable of sweeping the entire sky, resolving the disparity between targeted and all-sky observations. The data release also coincides with the eROSITA X-ray survey's second half-sky catalog, which nearly doubled the number of known X-ray sources to 2 million, and the two datasets are cross-referenced.

**Background**: Supermassive black holes are the largest type of black hole, with masses ranging from hundreds of thousands to billions of times the mass of the Sun. They grow by accreting cold gas from their host galaxies and can merge with other black holes when galaxies collide. Mapping them across the sky helps astronomers understand their distribution and evolution, which is linked to galaxy formation and large-scale structure of the universe.
