<summary><span class="hz-item-title">Inouye Telescope Observes Kelvin-Helmholtz Instability on Sun</span> <span class="hz-item-score">⭐️ 8.0/10</span></summary>

Scientists using the NSF Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope have directly observed Kelvin-Helmholtz instability (KHI) on the Sun's surface, a process theorized for decades. This marks the first direct observation of KHI in the solar atmosphere. This discovery is crucial for understanding how energy dissipates in the Sun, which is key to explaining sunspot and flare formation. It validates long-standing theories and demonstrates the power of the Inouye telescope in advancing solar physics. The observation was made by an international team from NSO, NSF NCAR High Altitude Observatory, and Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research, combining telescope data with computer simulations. The findings are published in an open-access Nature paper (s41586-026-10871-3).

🔗 [Source](https://nso.edu/press-release/nsf-inouye-solar-telescope-enables-major-discovery-of-a-hidden-solar-process/)

hackernews · neversaydie · Aug 5, 15:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49184355)

**Background**: Kelvin-Helmholtz instability occurs when there is velocity shear in a fluid or across the interface between two fluids, leading to characteristic swirling patterns. It is commonly seen in cloud formations on Earth and in Jupiter's atmosphere, but observing it on the Sun's surface has been challenging due to the need for high-resolution telescopes. The Inouye Solar Telescope, with its 4-meter mirror, is the world's largest solar telescope, providing unprecedented views of the Sun.
