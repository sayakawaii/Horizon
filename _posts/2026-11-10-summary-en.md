<summary><span class="hz-item-title">Semi-transparent magenta CdTe panels tested for agrivoltaics</span> <span class="hz-item-score">⭐️ 7.0/10</span></summary>

A peer-reviewed study in Cell Reports Physical Science evaluated semi-transparent, magenta-colored cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film photovoltaic panels for agrivoltaics, using frameless modules (model ASP-LAM2) from Advanced Solar Power (Hangzhou, China) with a colored PVB encapsulant layer. The research assesses land productivity when crops grow under these colored panels, which transmit selected light while generating electricity. Agrivoltaics aims to resolve land competition between solar farms and agriculture, and colored semi-transparent panels could make dual-use viable in greenhouses and high-value crop settings where full shade is unacceptable. If the approach proves economically sound, it could open new revenue streams for farmers and expand solar deployment without displacing food production. CdTe thin-film panels typically have lower efficiency than silicon (around 10.6% in some commercial products) and rely on tellurium, which is less abundant than cadmium. The magenta color comes from a PVB encapsulant layer, and the semi-transparent design trades electricity output for light transmission, so power yield falls roughly in proportion to the light allowed through.

hackernews · speckx · Nov 9, 14:31 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50426175)

**Background**: Agrivoltaics, also called agrisolar or dual-use solar, is the practice of using the same land for both solar energy and agriculture, with panels placed between crops, elevated above them, or on greenhouses. Because panels consume light, there are trade-offs between crop yield and energy production, though some shade-tolerant crops like leafy greens and ginger can benefit. Semi-transparent and colored solar cells are being developed specifically to let enough photosynthetically active light through for crops while still generating power.
