Registration with binfmt_misc can be done via a command like 'printf ... > /proc/sys/fs/binfmt_misc/register'.

Researchers introduced Quantization-Aware Healing (QAH), a technique that, when applied to a GPT-OSS 120B model compressed to 60B parameters and quantized to MXFP4, produces a model that outperforms its full-precision (bfloat16) version on 7 of 9 benchmarks. This breakthrough challenges the conventional trade-off between model compression and performance, showing that quantized models can not only match but exceed their full-precision counterparts. It offers a promising path for deploying efficient, high-performing AI models on resource-constrained hardware, potentially reducing costs and energy consumption in large-scale AI applications. The QAH recipe differs from standard quantization-aware training (QAT) by avoiding re-fitting to hard labels, which the authors found converges slowly and collapses past its peak. Instead, QAH recovers reasoning and coding capabilities in structurally compressed, 4-bit LLMs, achieving faster recovery than QAT.

🔗 [Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/MultiverseComputingCAI/quantization-aware-healing)

rss · Hugging Face Blog · Aug 25, 11:39

**Background**: Quantization is a model compression technique that reduces the precision of numerical values in a neural network, such as from 32-bit floating point to 4-bit integers, to decrease memory footprint and increase inference speed. Traditional quantization often leads to accuracy loss, and methods like quantization-aware training (QAT) aim to mitigate this by fine-tuning the quantized model. The new QAH approach offers an alternative that not only preserves but improves performance, as demonstrated on the GPT-OSS 120B model.
