A recent study suggests that taxi and ambulance drivers have lower mortality rates from Alzheimer's disease compared to the general population, possibly due to their reliance on spatial reasoning and navigation skills. The study reports a 40% lower chance of dying from Alzheimer's for these drivers. This finding adds to the growing evidence that engaging in complex cognitive tasks may protect against neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's. It could inform public health strategies and encourage activities that stimulate spatial reasoning as a preventive measure. The study compared mortality rates from Alzheimer's disease among taxi and ambulance drivers with other occupations, finding a significantly lower rate. However, critics point out that taxi drivers have a lower average life expectancy (around 67.8 years) compared to the general population (74 years), and since Alzheimer's is typically diagnosed around age 79, many taxi drivers may not live long enough to develop the disease. Additionally, selection bias may play a role, as individuals who become taxi drivers may already possess superior spatial abilities.
