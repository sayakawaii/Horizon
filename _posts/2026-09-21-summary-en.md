NASA's Mars Sample Return (MSR) mission, a joint Flagship-class effort with the European Space Agency, has been effectively cancelled as of 2026. The mission had been approved in 2022 to retrieve samples collected by the Perseverance rover, but cost growth and schedule slips led to its termination. The cancellation removes NASA's primary path to returning Martian samples for detailed laboratory analysis, potentially ceding leadership in Mars sample return to China's Tianwen-3 mission, which is targeting a 2028 launch and samples back by 2031. It also raises questions about the future of flagship planetary science missions and international collaboration in space exploration. The mission's cost had ballooned to an estimated $11 billion, with sample return delayed until 2040, and it was designed around legacy rockets like Ariane 64 rather than newer, lower-cost vehicles such as Starship or New Glenn. By comparison, the Apollo Moon missions returned 842 pounds of lunar rocks, while MSR was intended to bring back only about 1.1 pounds of Martian material.

**Background**: Mars Sample Return is a proposed multi-mission campaign to collect rock and dust samples on Mars and bring them to Earth, where they can be analyzed far more extensively than by onboard instruments, particularly to search for signs of past life. The NASA-ESA plan involved three elements: the Perseverance rover (already collecting samples), a sample retrieval lander with an ascent vehicle, and an Earth return orbiter. China's Tianwen-3 is a robotic dual-launch mission planned for the December 2028–January 2029 Mars launch window, aiming to return samples by 2031.
