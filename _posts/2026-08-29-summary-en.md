<summary><span class="hz-item-title">UrbanGround: Testing Multimodal LLM Agents' Navigation in a Real-Scale 3D City</span> <span class="hz-item-score">⭐️ 8.0/10</span></summary>

**Problem:** Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can interpret street views, but it is unclear whether such local perception remains useful for reliable action in complex urban environments. The paper addresses the gap in evaluating whether current MLLM agents can turn local perception into sustained goal-directed navigation in a real-scale city.

**Method:** The authors propose UrbanGround, the first sandbox built on a physically constrained 3D replica of Hong Kong from territory-wide geospatial data. It supports closed-loop first-person interaction and an interactive map for navigation, allowing agents to explore the 3D city. The analysis is structured around three research questions: local scene grounding, navigation with increasing destination distance and ambiguity, and robustness to route changes and pedestrian motion.

**Results:** Contemporary MLLM agents show useful atomic abilities in visual recognition and short-range spatial reasoning, but orientation and pedestrian-aware movement remain unreliable. Their central failure emerges over extended exploration, where local abilities do not compose into sustained goal-directed behavior and errors accumulate without effective correction.

**Significance:** UrbanGround provides a new benchmark to systematically study how far current MLLM agents can reliably explore complex, open-ended urban environments. It highlights the gap between atomic abilities and compositional navigation, guiding future research on embodied AI and urban autonomy.
